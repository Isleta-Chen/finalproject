# Sleep and Mental Health

**To: BST260 Teaching Team, Fall 2026**

## Introduction

For my final project, I would like to explore the relationship between
sleep and mental health. I am interested in this topic because sleep is
something that affects our daily lives, and I want to better understand
whether people who get less sleep also tend to have worse mental health.

I plan to look at this relationship in two different populations: adults
and high school students. I think it would be interesting to see whether
similar patterns appear in both groups, even though they are at
different stages of life.

## Research Questions

1.  Is shorter sleep duration associated with more severe depressive
    symptoms among U.S. adults?

2.  Are high school students who report insufficient sleep more likely
    to report poor mental health?

3.  Do these associations differ across demographic groups, including
    sex and age or grade level?

## Datasets

### Datasets 1:NHANES 2017–2018

For my first analysis, I plan to use the 2017–2018 National Health and
Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES), conducted by the CDC. I chose
this dataset because it includes information about sleep duration and
depressive symptoms. I plan to focus on adults aged 18 and older and
explore variables such as sleep duration, PHQ-9 depression scores, age,
and sex.

### Dataset 2: Youth Risk Behavior Survey (YRBS) 2023

For my second analysis, I would like to use the 2023 Youth Risk Behavior
Survey (YRBS), which collects information about health behaviors and
experiences among U.S. high school students. I want to use this dataset
to look at the relationship between sleep duration and self-reported
mental health. I am also interested in whether the patterns differ by
sex or grade level.

## Proposed Analysis

I plan to use R for data cleaning, descriptive statistics,
visualization, and exploratory statistical analysis.

First, I will summarize the distributions of sleep duration and mental
health indicators in each dataset. Then, I will compare mental health
outcomes across sleep-duration categories using tables and graphical
displays.

Also, I will use regression models to examine associations between sleep
duration and mental health outcomes while considering demographic
factors. I will account for relevant survey design features when
estimating population-level associations.

## Expected Outcomes and Limitations

I expect to identify patterns in the relationship between sleep duration
and mental health and to explore whether these patterns differ across
population groups.

Because both datasets are observational and cross-sectional, this study
will not establish causal relationships. Self-reported measures, missing
data, differences in mental health measurement, and potential
confounding may also affect interpretation.

The final project will present two separate analysis vignettes and
document the data sources, methods, findings, uncertainties, and
limitations.
